# Distill

Distill complex topics into clear, concise summaries for: **$ARGUMENTS**

## What This Skill Does

Take long documents, complex topics, technical concepts, or sprawling discussions and extract the essential — without losing what matters.

## Distillation Levels

Specify or infer from $ARGUMENTS:

- **Tweet** (280 chars): The single most important idea
- **TL;DR** (2-3 sentences): Core thesis + key implication
- **Executive Summary** (1 paragraph): What it is, why it matters, what to do
- **Key Points** (5 bullets): The must-knows for an informed person
- **Full Summary** (300-500 words): Complete understanding without reading the original

## Process

### 1. Identify the Core

Before summarizing, find:
- The central claim or argument (what is the author/document asserting?)
- The evidence or reasoning that supports it
- The conclusion or implication
- What would be lost if you cut it (keep) vs. what elaborates (cut)

### 2. Ruthless Prioritization

Apply the newspaper test: if a journalist were writing a story about this topic, what are the 3 facts they'd include? Those are your core.

Cut:
- Throat-clearing and background any reader can infer
- Repetition of the same point in different words
- Caveats and hedges that don't change the conclusion
- Examples that illustrate but aren't load-bearing

### 3. Structural Choices

- Lead with the conclusion (readers don't have to work to find the point)
- Use the original's language for key terms (don't introduce synonyms that confuse)
- Preserve the hierarchy: main point → supporting points → caveats

### 4. Verify Completeness

A good distillation passes this test: if someone read only your summary, could they make the same decision as someone who read the full original? If no, what's missing?

## Output

Provide the requested distillation level clearly labeled. If the request is ambiguous, deliver both a TL;DR and a bulleted key points version.
