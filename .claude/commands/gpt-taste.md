# GPT Taste

Apply considered AI-era aesthetic taste to content and design decisions for: **$ARGUMENTS**

## What This Skill Does

Distinguish between content and design that feels thoughtfully AI-assisted vs. content that feels generated — and help produce the former.

## The Problem This Solves

AI-generated content and design has developed recognizable patterns that signal "unreviewed output":
- Excessive use of em dashes and colons
- Lists of 3 things when 2 or 5 would be more natural
- "Delve into", "tapestry of", "nuanced", "it's worth noting"
- Headers for every paragraph in copy that doesn't need navigation
- Perfectly balanced pros/cons that refuse to take a position
- Responses that start with "Certainly!" or restate the question

Good taste in the AI era means: using AI for speed and breadth, then applying human judgment for specificity, opinion, and surprise.

## Evaluation Criteria

For $ARGUMENTS, evaluate against:

### Voice and Specificity

- Does this sound like a specific person with specific opinions?
- Are claims specific ("42% faster") or vague ("significantly improved")?
- Does it take a clear position, or hedge every statement?

### Structure That Earns Its Complexity

- Are headers used because navigation is needed, or just to look organized?
- Is the list format because information is parallel, or because it's the default?
- Does the length match the importance of the content?

### Language Patterns to Purge

- "In today's fast-paced world" / "In the ever-evolving landscape"
- "It's important to note that" / "It's worth mentioning"
- "This allows you to" (just say what it does)
- Adjective pileup: "comprehensive, holistic, innovative solution"
- Fake transitions: "With that said," / "That being said,"

## Output

For $ARGUMENTS: identify the AI-pattern tells present, rewrite the most egregious examples, and deliver a version that feels human-reviewed, opinionated, and specific.
