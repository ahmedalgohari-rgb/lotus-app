# Redesign Existing Projects

Plan and direct redesign strategy for existing products for: **$ARGUMENTS**

## What This Skill Does

Guide a product redesign from diagnosis through execution — preserving what works, fixing what doesn't, and managing the risks that kill most redesigns.

## Why Redesigns Fail (Avoid These)

- **The blank slate trap**: throwing away what works because it feels old
- **The committee design**: trying to please everyone, standing for nothing
- **The big bang launch**: releasing the full redesign at once instead of incrementally
- **Losing the SEO**: redesigning URLs, removing content, breaking internal links
- **Ignoring muscle memory**: your most loyal users have habits — break them at your peril

## Redesign Process

### 1. Audit Before Designing

Understand what exists before changing it:
- What does quantitative data say? (top pages, conversion paths, drop-off points)
- What does qualitative research say? (user interviews, session recordings, support tickets)
- What are the top 5 user complaints about the current design?
- What are the top 5 things users love about the current design? (don't touch these)

### 2. Define the Redesign Goals

Be specific:
- "Improve conversion on the pricing page from 2.1% to 3.5%"
- "Reduce support tickets about [feature] by 50%"
- "Pass WCAG AA accessibility compliance"
- NOT "make it look more modern"

Without a measurable goal, there's no way to know if the redesign succeeded.

### 3. Scope the Redesign

Classify each area:
- **Preserve**: works well, high traffic, don't touch
- **Improve**: functional but underperforming, targeted changes
- **Redesign**: broken or missing, fundamental rethink needed
- **Remove**: nobody uses it, adds confusion, kill it

### 4. Incremental Release Strategy

Ship in phases, not all at once:
- Phase 1: New design system / tokens (invisible to users)
- Phase 2: Redesigned high-traffic pages (measured against old versions)
- Phase 3: Feature redesigns (with feature flags for rollback)
- Phase 4: Navigation and global elements (most disruptive, do last)

### 5. Communication Plan

For users who will experience change:
- What changes, when
- Why (brief, honest, user-benefit framing)
- How to get help if something is broken
- Feedback channel

For $ARGUMENTS: audit framework, redesign goals, scope classification, phased release plan, and risk list with mitigation strategies.
