# Image Taste — Frontend

Direct image selection and treatment for web and mobile interfaces: **$ARGUMENTS**

## What This Skill Does

Make the calls a designer with strong image taste would make — which images to use, how to crop and treat them, and how to integrate photography and illustration into UI without the interface falling apart.

## The Problem With Images in UI

Most UI image problems come from:
- **Inconsistency** — every photo has different tone, subject, framing
- **Text legibility** — dark text on busy photos, or vice versa
- **Aspect ratio chaos** — photos fighting their containers
- **Stock photo stench** — business people handshaking, diverse teams pointing at whiteboards
- **Wrong emotional register** — high-energy photo on a calm product, or vice versa

## Image Selection Criteria

For a coherent visual system, every image should share:

**Subject consistency**
- Human presence: always / sometimes / never (pick one and commit)
- Environment: indoor / outdoor / abstract (establish a rule)
- Framing: wide / mid / close (varies by use case, but not randomly)

**Color treatment**
- Does the image palette fit the brand palette?
- Warm or cool bias? (don't mix both in the same section)
- Saturation level: rich and vivid or desaturated and editorial?
- Should images be treated with an overlay or filter for brand cohesion?

**Photography style**
- Documentary / candid (real moments, authentic) vs. styled / directed (perfect, controlled)
- High contrast / dramatic vs. soft / approachable
- Busy/textured backgrounds vs. clean/minimal

## Image Treatment for UI

### Overlays
When placing text over images:
- Dark overlay (gradient from bottom): text at bottom of image
- Color overlay with brand color at 40-60% opacity: creates cohesion across different source images
- Blur + frosted glass: preserves image interest while ensuring text legibility

### Cropping Rules
- Faces: crop at forehead or chin, never at eyes or mouth
- People: respect the "action space" (leave room in the direction they face)
- Products: consistent margins, no clipping of key product features
- Landscapes: rule of thirds for horizon placement

### Responsive Behavior
- Specify focal point for each image (center-top, center, center-bottom)
- Test every image at both 16:9 and 1:1 crop
