# Quieter

Make content more subtle, restrained, and sophisticated for: **$ARGUMENTS**

## What This Skill Does

Dial back content that's trying too hard — removing the noise, the urgency theater, and the overselling until what remains is genuinely confident and considered.

## When Quieter is the Right Move

Some audiences respond to restraint, not amplification:
- High-income or executive buyers (they're sold to constantly — they respect the un-sell)
- Design-conscious audiences (sophistication reads as quality)
- B2B with long sales cycles (trust builds through credibility, not enthusiasm)
- Premium or luxury positioning (loudness is incompatible with premium)

## What to Remove

**Enthusiasm markers that undermine credibility**
- Exclamation points (one per page maximum, earn it)
- "Amazing," "incredible," "revolutionary," "game-changing"
- ALL CAPS for emphasis (use italics or structure instead)
- Multiple emojis or emojis in professional contexts

**Urgency theater**
- Countdown timers that reset
- "Limited time only" with no actual limit
- "Act now" with no reason to act now
- Manufactured scarcity

**Social proof overreach**
- Unverifiable claims ("the world's best")
- Superlatives without evidence ("fastest," "easiest," "most powerful")
- Testimonials that feel planted or generic

**Visual noise**
- Cluttered layouts with no breathing room
- Too many CTAs competing
- Gradient abuse
- Decorative elements that don't carry meaning

## What Quieter Sounds Like

Replace → with:
- "Amazing results" → "Here's what happened" (then let the numbers speak)
- "You need to try this" → "[Product] does [thing]" (declarative, not imperative)
- "Don't miss out!" → [Remove. Say nothing if you have nothing to say.]
- "The best in the industry" → "[Specific differentiator]" (what specifically, not superlative)

Quieter writing:
- Trusts the reader to draw their own conclusions from good evidence
- Uses periods more than exclamation points
- Lets data speak instead of adjectives
- Doesn't repeat itself to be sure you got the point

## Output

Rewrite $ARGUMENTS in a quieter register. Show what was removed and why. The test: does the rewritten version feel more trustworthy and authoritative than the original?
