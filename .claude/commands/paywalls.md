# Paywalls

Design paywall strategy, messaging, and conversion optimization for: **$ARGUMENTS**

## What This Skill Does

Design the paywall moment — what triggers it, what it says, and how it converts without destroying trust or goodwill.

## Paywall Philosophy

The best paywalls feel like a natural next step, not a roadblock. The user should think "of course I need to upgrade to do that" — not "this is annoying."

This happens when:
- The free tier is genuinely valuable (they've gotten real value before the wall)
- The paywall appears at a natural capability boundary (not an arbitrary limit)
- The upgrade message reminds them of value already received, not just what they'll get

## Paywall Types and When to Use Each

**Hard paywall** — premium content or features, completely locked
Use when: the feature is the core differentiator, the free tier is clearly defined

**Soft paywall / limit-based** — free up to X uses, then prompt
Use when: the feature is valuable but shareable, you want users to experience it before paying

**Feature differentiation** — free tier has subset of features, paid has all
Use when: there's a natural segmentation between casual and power users

**Content paywall** — article limit per month (metered)
Use when: content is the product, not the software

## Paywall Copy Framework

### The Moment Before

The screens leading up to the paywall:
- Show the value of what they're about to unlock (preview)
- Don't surprise them — telegraph that this is a paid feature
- For limit-based: show remaining uses so the wall isn't unexpected

### The Paywall Screen

**Headline**: Name the specific thing they want to do
- "To [do the thing they just tried], upgrade to Pro"
- NOT: "Upgrade your plan"

**Value recap**: Remind them what they've already gotten
- "You've [done X actions] with [product]. Pro unlocks [specific next-level thing]."

**The offer**: Clear pricing, clear features, clear risk reversal
- Most important: make the price feel small relative to the value
- Risk reversal: "Cancel anytime," "7-day free trial," "Money-back guarantee"

**CTA**: Specific and benefit-focused
- "Start my free trial" > "Upgrade now" > "Get Pro"

**Escape hatch**: Let them continue on free gracefully
- "Continue with free" or "Maybe later" — no dark patterns

### Objection Handling

Preempt the 3 most common objections:
1. Price: show value relative to cost
2. Risk: free trial or money-back
3. Timing: save progress, pick up later
