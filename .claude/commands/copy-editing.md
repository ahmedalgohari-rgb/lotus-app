# Copy Editing

Edit and improve existing copy for clarity, impact, and correctness: **$ARGUMENTS**

## What This Skill Does

Go beyond proofreading — restructure, tighten, and strengthen copy while preserving the author's voice and intent.

## Editing Layers

Work through these in order (don't jump to line edits before structural issues are resolved):

### Layer 1 — Structure Edit

- Is the argument or narrative logical?
- Does the lede bury the most important point?
- Are sections in the right order?
- Is there redundancy that can be cut entirely?
- Does each paragraph have one clear job?

### Layer 2 — Paragraph Edit

- Does each paragraph open with a topic sentence?
- Are transitions between paragraphs smooth and necessary?
- Is the length varied to control pace? (Mix short and long)

### Layer 3 — Sentence Edit

- Kill passive voice where active is cleaner
- Split sentences over 25 words
- Remove redundant pairs: "each and every", "first and foremost"
- Replace nominalization: "make a decision" → "decide"
- Front-load important information in each sentence

### Layer 4 — Word Edit

- Cut filler: "very", "really", "quite", "just", "that"
- Replace vague words with specific ones
- Ensure consistency of terminology throughout
- Check for jargon the audience won't know

### Layer 5 — Proofreading

- Grammar, spelling, punctuation
- Consistent capitalization and hyphenation
- Factual claims that can be verified
- Formatting consistency (headers, bullets, spacing)

## Output Format

Provide:
1. **Edited version** — the full rewritten copy
2. **Change log** — bullet list of significant changes made and why
3. **Flagged items** — anything that needs the author's judgment (factual claims, tone choices, missing info)

For short copy (under 200 words): show tracked changes inline using `~~strikethrough~~` for deletions and **bold** for additions.
