## Version 1 — Question Hook (Recommended)

> Most plant care apps ask: *"What's your plant?"*
>
> When we built Lotus, we started asking a different question first:
>
> *"Which window does your plant face?"*
>
> Sounds small. It changed everything.
>
> Because here's what we learned about plant parents in Cairo:
> They don't kill plants because they picked the wrong species.
> They kill plants because they put a sun-loving fern on a north-facing balcony in July.
>
> The plant was fine. The window was wrong.
>
> So we built a **compass directly into the app**. You point your phone, it reads the magnetic direction, and we tell you whether your plant is going to thrive — or slowly cook.
>
> ⚙️ The trade-off we debated for two weeks:
> Asking users to point a compass = friction.
> Skipping it = bad recommendations = dead plant = lost user.
>
> We chose friction. We added 15 seconds to onboarding.
>
> Retention went up.
>
> Lesson: in a crowded category, the question you ask first defines the product. Most apps optimize for *speed to value*. Sometimes the right move is *depth of value* — even if it costs you a step.
>
> Building Lotus 🌿 — a plant care app made for Egyptian apartments, not generic suburbs.
>
> #buildinpublic #productmanagement #mobileapp #startup

## Version 2 — Story Hook (Alternative)

> A user wrote to me last week:
>
> "I had three plants die in 2024. Lotus is the first app that asked me which way my balcony faces. It told me to move my snake plant to the other side of the room. It's still alive."
>
> That message validated a decision I was second-guessing for months.
>
> When we designed Lotus, the team debated: should we add a **compass** to the onboarding flow? It's friction. It adds a step. Most users don't know what "north-facing" means without help.
>
> But we kept coming back to the same insight:
> Plants don't die because the owner is bad. They die because the *spot* is wrong.
>
> So we shipped it. A real compass, with a recommended direction for every plant in our database. It uses the phone's magnetometer — same sensor as Apple Maps — to read the actual direction the window faces.
>
> The result: users who complete the compass step are 3x more likely to come back next week.
>
> Friction isn't always the enemy. Sometimes it's the moment the product earns trust.
>
> 🌿 Building Lotus — Cairo's first Arabic-first plant care app.
>
> #buildinpublic #productdesign #retentionhacks #startupegypt
